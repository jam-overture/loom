# Building with Loom

This section is about the half of a Loom page that you write.

The other half is the runtime, and it is the same for everybody. What is yours is
the vocabulary: the components a page may be built from, the props each of them
accepts, how they hold each other, where their words and numbers come from, and
which of them an AI model is allowed to touch. Nothing in Loom knows any of that
until you say it.

## The pages in this section

[Primitives and the registry](/docs/building-with-loom/primitives) is the one to
read first: everything else here assumes you know what those two words mean.
After it, the pages can be read in any order.

## The trade you are making

A page here can only name components you registered, with props they declared.
That is a real loss. You give up building a page out of anything you like, and
what you get back is a change somebody can read before it happens: a list of
named operations against named components, rather than a diff of generated
markup.

The second half of the same trade is worth saying as plainly: **behavior lives in
the component, not in the tree.** A tree can say which component goes where and
what text it carries. It cannot say what happens when the button is pressed.
That is your code, in your repository, under review the way the rest of your code
is.
