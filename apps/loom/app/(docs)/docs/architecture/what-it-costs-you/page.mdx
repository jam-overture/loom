# What it costs you

Every framework is a shape, and a shape is mostly refusals. The rest of this
site is about what Loom lets you do. This page is the other column, and it is
the one to read before you commit anything to it.

## The bargain, said once more

Loom lets an AI change a page that is already running, and the reason that is
not alarming is that it can only say a small number of things. The page is
data. A change is data. The words a change may use are a list you wrote.

**You give up the freedom to have the AI produce anything at all, and what you
get back is a change you can read, measure, refuse and undo.** The other half of
the same trade: what a thing *does* lives in the component you registered, never
in the tree — so a proposal can move your checkout, rename it and take it away,
and it can never change what happens when somebody presses the button.

Everything below is that trade, itemized.

## Eight things you can no longer do

Under each one is the ruling that made it so, and what that ruling turned down
instead. Those lists are read out of the records as this page builds — they are
not a summary of them, and they never say *why*. The why is in the record, one
click away, written on the day. Where a ruling has since been replaced in part,
the box says so, because part of it is somebody else's now.

## What is not settled

The eight above will read the same next year. Some of what Loom has turned down
will not, because a record sometimes declines to close a question rather than
answering it — *deferred*, *rejected for now*, *worth revisiting once*. Those
are the constraints that might lift, and there is no reason to make you dig for
them.

A row is here because the record's own paragraph says so, in the sentence
printed under it. Nothing is judged for it: if a ruling changes its mind, this
list changes with it, and if it never does, the row stays.

## The cost nobody records

There is one more, and it is not in any of the rulings because it is not a
decision anybody made: **Loom is worth its price only if the same page changes
more than once.** A page that ships and then sits there gets the whole bill —
the registry, the policy, the store, the log — and none of the benefit, which
only arrives the fourth time somebody asks for something and nobody has to open
an editor.

That is a judgement about your situation rather than about the framework, and it
is the one thing on this page that no test can hold true.

## Where the arguments are

[Decision records](/docs/architecture/decision-records) is every ruling Loom has
made, in the order they were made, with what each one replaced. The alternatives
named on this page are all in there with their reasoning attached, and so are
the several hundred that no cost above points at.
