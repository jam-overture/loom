# Decision records

A decision record is a short note written on the day something was settled. It
says what the situation was, what was decided, what that costs — and which
sensible alternative was rejected, and why.

That last part is the one worth having. A codebase shows you what was built. It
never shows you the three things that were nearly built instead, so the next
person to have the same good idea has to rediscover on their own why it does not
work. The records are where that is kept.

Each one has a number, which is just its filename and the order it was written
in. **Nowhere else on this site will you be shown one**, because four digits are
a footnote to a document you have not opened. Here they are the subject, so
here they are.

## The trail is never edited

When Loom changes direction, the old record is not corrected. It is marked as
replaced, its text left exactly as it was, and a new record explains what changed
and why the earlier reasoning no longer holds.

So a record can be in one of a few states, and the wording in the table below is
the record's own:

| State | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Accepted** | In force. This is how Loom works today. |
| **Accepted — partially superseded by …** | Still in force, except for the one part a later record took over. A record that answered several questions can be replaced in part; retiring the whole thing would lose more than it clarified. |
| **Superseded by …** | Replaced. Worth reading anyway — the reasoning that stopped holding is usually the interesting bit. |
| **Proposed** | Written, not settled. Waiting on a person. |

<Callout kind="note">
  A change that contradicts an accepted record is an escalation, not a
  refactor. The replacement is written as **Proposed**, the existing record
  stays standing, and somebody decides. That is why the list below is nearly all
  *Accepted* — it is a trail, not a backlog.
</Callout>
