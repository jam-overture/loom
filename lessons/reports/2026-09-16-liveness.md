## Why liveness, and why it is Part V

[0138](../../decisions/0138-a-queue-can-be-told-which-of-its-holds-are-already-dead.md)
— written 7 September, renumbered on the 12th when its branch merged,
`src/write/liveness.ts` untouched since it landed. Nine days settled, `Accepted`,
and nothing in the file has moved.

It earns Part V membership on a third axis, and the README now says which:

- Lessons 18–23: a fact the runtime cannot check because **somebody else owns
  it** — a host's registry, a deployment's theme, another document's anchors.
- Lesson 24: the same, except **nobody has declared it yet**, which on the day a
  seam ships is every party there is.
- Lesson 25: a fact **nobody owns**, unavailable because of what a type system is.
- Lesson 26: a fact **two parties own**, both inside Loom, both correct, both
  willing to answer — and it still has no home, because the two were deliberately
  built not to know about each other.

A hold records the revision it was judged against. A tree records the revision it
is at. Neither is missing anything. `forTree` and `waiting` do not read the tree
store, which is [0020](../../decisions/0020-a-store-handle-is-the-scope-and-a-listing-is-a-page.md)
— a store handle is the scope. So the fact that decides whether a waiting change
can still be applied is in neither store, and the review queue the documentation
tells you to build lists dead changes and live ones oldest-first and
indistinguishable.

**Nothing needs declaring here.** That is what makes it a different lesson rather
than a fifth example: the remedy is a *function*, and the design question becomes
who is allowed to hold two stores next to each other and what they owe you when
they could only reach one.

## What I emphasised, and why

**1. The field you want to add is the wrong half of the comparison.** The first
fix everybody proposes is `headRevision` on the hold row, written at hold time.
It is exactly backwards, and saying why is the most transferable paragraph in the
lesson: the hold *already* carries a revision, and that number is correct forever;
the number the queue needs is the other one, whose defining property is that it
moves after the hold is written. A field written at hold time is a snapshot of a
value whose whole job is to change. Explain it back (1) asks for this **without
the word "stale"**, because "stale" makes it sound like an implementation detail
rather than a category error.

**2. The direction, and the authority.** `headRevision === hold.baseRevision`,
not `>`. The case that separates them is a hold naming a revision *ahead* of head
— which a log cannot produce and a restored backup can — and the reason is not
about revisions at all:

> A derived predicate is correct when it agrees with the authority, not when it
> agrees with the story you would tell about the data.

The authority is `commit.ts:312`, five lines of context away, and it is an
inequality. "The page has moved past where this was judged" is a story. Predict 2
makes the reader write the expression and *then* evaluate the third case, which
is the order in which most people discover their expression has already answered
for them.

**3. The third answer, and the test I want readers to take away.** `unknown`
rather than an optimistic `live`, and the module's own sentence is the reason:

> A queue that shows no badge is honest; one that shows *live* on a tree nobody
> could reach is the failure this module exists to remove, restated one level up.

Generalised in the lesson as: **does the fix contain the defect?** — which it
usually does when the fix has to report on itself and you gave it a default. This
is the rule I most want to survive the lesson, and exercise F prints its cost in
rows rather than arguing it.

**4. Declining the neighbouring question.** `not-found` is `unknown` and not
`dead`, although every downstream consequence is identical. The argument is about
what a badge *claims* rather than what it causes: *the page moved on*, about a
page that is gone, is a true-sounding sentence about the wrong fault. Predict 4
asks the reader to write their badge text **and read it out loud**, which is a
different test from writing a type and is the one that catches this.

**5. A module about partial knowledge may not have a total failure mode.**
`markHoldsFromStore` returns no `Result`, and 0138 says a `Result` was the first
shape and the wrong one. Fail-fast would make the module's own point — *say what
you cannot say rather than guessing* — unavailable in exactly the case it exists
for. The lesson also names what the chosen shape costs, which the record does
not: `unreadable` is a list a careless caller can ignore, and then the queue has
unexplained blank badges instead of an error. That is the right way round, and
saying so is better than pretending there was no trade.

## The exercises

Six, all executed against this checkout's `src/` before anything was written
down, by putting the Try it fences into `src/scratch.test.ts` in document order
and running `pnpm vitest run src/scratch.test.ts`. The transcripts in the lesson
are that run's. `src/scratch.test.ts` is deleted. `transcripts.test.ts` now
recognises six more blocks and every one matched the page runner's own output.

**A — the queue, built the documented way.** Three holds, two pages, and the last
line prints the field names:

```
everything a row carries: proposalId, treeId, baseRevision, intent, proposal, disposition, heldAt
```

Seven fields, every one true when the hold was written and still true now, and
none of them the one the queue needs. This is the whole defect printed rather
than described, and it is why the exercise prints `Object.keys` rather than a
prose list of them.

**B — the comparison in its four positions.**

```
head 4, judged against 4: live
head 5, judged against 4: dead
head 4, judged against 5: dead
no head to compare with:  unknown
```

The third line is the lesson. The fourth is a design decision compressed into one
word.

**C — a page marked against heads the caller already has.** Four in, four out,
order kept, one `unknown`. The assertion I care about is *four in, four out*: a
marker that dropped the rows it had no head for would produce a queue correct
about everything it showed and quietly shorter than the truth, which is the same
defect wearing the opposite mask.

**D — reading the heads, including the one that is not there.** The documentation
lane's terminal session, in one program: two pages created, one ordinary change
lands on the second, and a hold against a page that was never created.

```
t_home judged against 0, head 0 -> live
t_pricing judged against 0, head 1 -> dead
t_deleted judged against 0, head unread -> unknown
tally: live=1 dead=1 unknown=1
heads it could not read: not-found
```

Both halves of the `not-found` classification are visible at once: the row says
`unknown` and the `why` is handed back separately, in the store's vocabulary
rather than the badge's.

**E — one question per tree.** Five rows, two reads, and a `TreeReader` double
whose `revisions` throws rather than returning an empty page — a double that
fails loudly instead of agreeing quietly, which the lesson stops to point at.

**F — the two answers that were not taken, costed.**

```
as built:  live=1 dead=1 unknown=1
unknown read as live: offers 2 of 3
unknown read as dead: offers 1 of 3
```

Optimistic offers two and one of them cannot be answered — the original defect,
rebuilt inside the fix. Pessimistic offers one and hides a change that may be
fine, which is a smaller lie and a worse one in a way the number does not show: a
store unreachable all afternoon looks identical to an afternoon when nothing
needed reviewing.

**One transcript in this lesson was wrong when I first wrote it, and I caught it
by reading the run rather than the draft.** Exercise F's fourth printed line is
the legend's `live` row; I had written a version where the legend's sentence
appears under the label *"unknown read as live"*, and built a paragraph on top of
that reading. The program prints no such line. The paragraph is gone and the
transcript is the run's. This is the second time the exercise discipline has
caught a lesson inventing the more interesting output, and both times the
invented one was better writing and false.

## Found while teaching

**One finding filed, for `Loom daily build`. Nothing fixed; `src/` is not this
lane's.** It came out of the ceiling research rather than out of lesson 26, which
is the only reason there is one at all.

`src/deadline.ts` guards the losing promise with `answered.catch(() => undefined)`
and the module comment says the module *"never leaves a rejection unhandled"*.
`Promise.race` subscribes to every promise it is handed, synchronously, so a
rejection arriving after the race is decided already has a handler. The explicit
catch is defensive rather than load-bearing on this path.

Measured, by deleting the line from a working copy:

| suite | without the line |
| --- | --- |
| `src/deadline.test.ts` (11 tests, including *"does not leave a late rejection unhandled"*) | passes |
| the three seam suites that use the ceiling (79 tests) | passes |

Both reverted; `git status` was clean before this branch committed anything.

**It is not a defect and I filed it as one that is not.** The line is cheap and I
would keep it — it becomes load-bearing the moment somebody replaces
`Promise.race` with a hand-rolled resolver. What is worth changing is the *test*,
because the repository contains a test whose name states a property, which passes,
and which would pass identically if the mechanism it is named for were deleted.
That is lesson 25's weakest-row problem in a new place: the second source is the
same promise semantics that would have made the test pass anyway. Three shapes
offered, none picked.

## What is next

system gives an incomplete answer, what does the incomplete part look like on a
screen — and is there anybody whose job it is to notice that it is there?**

That is deliberately a question about a surface rather than about `src/`, because
three of the last four lessons have ended pointing at the runtime and the
interesting unexamined seam may be the other way. `unreadable` in `MarkedHolds` is
